All 9 cookies on bradleyco.com fire before a visitor can take any action — including 6 Google Analytics and 2 ZoomInfo tracking cookies classified as analytical, plus 1 unclassified ZoomInfo identifier. No consent management platform exists. Twelve U.S. states (CA, CO, CT, DE, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, TX) now legally require websites to detect and honor Global Privacy Control signals automatically. These laws apply based on where the visitor is located, not where the company operates — any U.S.-facing site with tracking technology is in scope. California has issued seven-figure fines for exactly this configuration; Ford Motor Company was required in March 2026 to audit every tracking technology on its properties for GPC non-compliance.

!
GPC Compliance Gap — CA, CO, CT, DE, MD, MN, MT, NE, NH, NJ, OR, and TX require automatic GPC signal recognition. Visitors from these states currently receive no opt-out protection on bradleyco.com. A CookieYes or OneTrust implementation resolves this for $150/mo.

Why Specific Pages Beat General Pages

A single “Commercial Real Estate” homepage cannot simultaneously rank for “commercial real estate Indianapolis,” “commercial real estate Grand Rapids,” and “office space for rent Indianapolis” — because Google and AI engines match a specific question to a specific answer. This is how AI search works: a prompt like “commercial real estate firm in Indianapolis” triggers geo-specific and service-specific sub-searches behind the scenes, and the engine surfaces whichever pages answer each piece most directly. A dedicated Indianapolis commercial real estate page gives Google and ChatGPT one clear, specific answer for that search instead of asking them to extract the Indianapolis context from a national services page. The same logic applies to each Bradley office market: one page per city, built around what buyers in that city are actually searching for.
